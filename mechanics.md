# Mechanics proposal for a persistent solo campaign

**Status: planning proposal. No campaign has started.** All numbers and game procedures here are house rules. This document does not claim that the novels use these rules.

The design assumes a new adult novice, a standard route through Accepted to Aes Sedai, and a target of 40–60 substantial sessions. The player can pursue other goals. No single ally, random result, hidden Potential value or optional subplot can close the earned rank route.

## 1 Resolve uncertain actions

Use one test when an action is uncertain and has a material consequence:

**2d10 + one Attribute + one Skill + one situational modifier.**

| Total | Result |
|---|---|
| At least the difficulty | Clean success. Achieve the stated goal. |
| 1–3 below the difficulty | Achieve the stated goal with the stated cost, or make useful progress when the goal needs several steps. |
| At least 4 below the difficulty | Setback. Apply the stated consequence and change the situation. |

Normal difficulty is **14**. A hard task uses **18**. An exceptional task uses **22**. Safe routine work succeeds without a roll. An impossible action needs a different method; it does not need repeated rolls.

Before a roll, state the goal, difficulty, ability, modifier and possible cost. The player can change the plan. The GM must not increase difficulty merely because the character improved. A task retains its difficulty when its actual conditions stay the same.

Good preparation or suitable help gives **+2**. A material obstacle gives **−2**. Apply each at most once; they cancel each other. Multiple allies or preparations do not stack the bonus. Attributes and Skills give at most +7; situational help can raise the total bonus to +9.

For an opposed action outside conflict, use difficulty `10 + the defender's relevant Attribute + Skill`. Roll only for the player character. Meeting the difficulty is a clean success; there is no separate tied roll. Published Guard replaces this formula during conflict. One character leads a group action. Other participants can provide the single permitted assistance bonus.

Do not repeat a test against an unchanged situation. A setback must reveal information, spend a stated resource, advance a threat or create another route. Use at most three decisive tests for a normal scene. A long fight can use up to six before the GM offers a changed situation or a clear escape opportunity. These are pacing limits, not automatic victories.

## 2 Starting character

Assign Attribute ratings **2, 2, 1, 1, 0**. Ratings range from 0 to 3.

| Attribute | Use |
|---|---|
| Body | Strength and physical endurance |
| Grace | Balance, speed and precise movement |
| Wits | Observation, reasoning and memory |
| Will | Focus and resistance to pressure |
| Presence | Social influence and leadership |

Choose two Skills at **2** and four Skills at **1**. All other Skills start at 0. Ratings range from 0 to 4.

| Skill | Normal Attribute | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Athletics | Body or Grace | Climb, swim, run |
| Melee | Body or Grace | Staff, sword, close combat |
| Marksmanship | Grace | Bow and thrown weapons |
| Stealth | Grace | Hide and move quietly |
| Survival | Wits | Track, navigate, make camp |
| Notice | Wits | Find a visible clue or immediate danger |
| Scholarship | Wits | Research, languages and recorded knowledge |
| Medicine | Wits | First aid and ordinary treatment |
| Persuasion | Presence | Negotiate, deceive or make a case |
| Insight | Wits | Interpret behaviour and motives |
| Leadership | Presence | Coordinate and steady a group |
| Composure | Will | Stay focused under fear, pain or pressure |

An unfamiliar language or specialist fact needs a source. A high Scholarship rating does not grant all knowledge. Insight reveals behaviour; it does not read thoughts. Use a different Attribute only when the player describes a method that uses it.

**Example novice:** Body 1, Grace 1, Wits 2, Will 2, Presence 0. Scholarship 2, Composure 2, Melee 1, Notice 1, Medicine 1 and Persuasion 1. This character has an ordinary melee bonus of +2, not the +4 of a trained novice fighter.

Start at **Level 1**, with full Vitality, no Wounds, **4 Supply**, **6 silver coins**, Control 1 and the channeling ratings below. Maximum Vitality is **6 + 2 × Body**; the example novice starts with 8. Basic clothes, a staff, a knife, a cloak, a notebook and a water container are included. Gear can change during character creation without a point cost if it has the same function.

All resource amounts are nonnegative integers. Clamp Vitality to 0–maximum, Wounds to 0–3, Supply to 0–6 and Strain to 0–twice Current Capacity. Recovery cannot exceed a maximum. Spending cannot make a resource negative. Reject an invalid action before spending time or resources. Round required time and quantities up; round fractional recovery down.

## 3 General advancement

There are **12 chapters** and **12 general levels**. Complete chapters 1–11 to gain one level and **4 General Points** each time. Chapter 12 is the concluding assessment and resolution; it does not create Level 13. The player has earned **44 General Points** by Level 12.

Publish each chapter's completion conditions before play. Each chapter needs three or four different proofs. Examples include resolving a field problem, demonstrating a skill, making a consequential choice and closing an investigation. Alternative methods can satisfy a proof. Repeated checks, extra messages, repeated sparring and repeated easy tasks give no points.

- Raise a Skill by one: pay its **new rating** in General Points.
- Raise an Attribute by one: pay **3 × its new rating**.
- Buy each intervening rating. Keep unused points.
- Chapter proofs include general practice. Spend points at a safe chapter-end boundary without an extra time charge.

Banked General Points remain available at later chapter boundaries. Describe the relevant practice, but do not add a random training gate. Weave learning and bridging instruction retain their separate time costs.

An exact 44-point example raises the novice to Will 3, Body 2 and Presence 1. Skills become Scholarship 4, Composure 4, Melee 3, Notice 2, Medicine 2, Persuasion 2 and Survival 1. The other ratings stay unchanged. This is one viable build, not a required build.

There is no point award for an arbitrary chat boundary. A substantial session means about 60–120 minutes of active play, or an equivalent asynchronous sequence. It includes meaningful choices and a useful stopping point. A session can contain many messages or resume across several conversations.

After the shawl, Level stays at 12. A completed new chapter can award 4 General Points, 2 Channel Points and two weave-learning opportunities. The same rating caps apply. New knowledge, allies, projects, responsibilities and rare weave discoveries also provide advancement. None is required to preserve an already earned rank. Potential 9 or 10 permits a further capacity proof for each unused point; Potential 8 still supports a complete post-shawl campaign.

## 4 Channeling tracks

**Potential** is the fixed ceiling on Current Capacity. At setup, roll one fair d3 and add 7. Each result, 8, 9 or 10, has probability 1/3. Save the die and its result before they can affect play. Never draw again or change the result to fit the plot. It remains hidden until discovery, unless the player requests disclosure. All three values support the full rank route.

**Current Capacity** starts at 3. It rises to 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8 after named capacity proofs in chapters 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10. A proof is a safe supervised demonstration or another published equivalent. It is not a random breakthrough. Capacity cannot exceed Potential. Capacity 9 or 10 is optional later growth; the core campaign never requires it.

**Control** is a trained rating from 1 to 4. It governs safe execution, and it replaces an ordinary Skill in a channeling test: **2d10 + Will + Control**.

**Flow ratings** measure training with Air, Earth, Fire, Water and Spirit. They are not a claim that innate strength can be trained without limit. Each Flow starts at 1; choose one to start at 2. Flow ratings range from 1 to 3. They are prerequisites for weaves and do not add to a test.

Chapters 1–10 each award **2 Channel Points**, separate from General Points. Raising Control or one Flow by one costs its new rating. Buy each intervening rating. Later chapters can award Channel Points if the campaign continues, but qualification does not depend on them.

**Weave knowledge** is separate again. The character starts knowing Spark and Air Hand. Each completed chapter permits learning up to two available weaves, if their prerequisites are met. Each takes one week of downtime with a suitable source. No additional point cost or random learning roll applies. The curriculum always provides a source for required forms. Rare optional weaves can require an adventure.

Some weaves need special talents or discoveries. Mark those prerequisites explicitly. No optional talent can be a hidden requirement for the ordinary rank route.

### Casting and strain

Every weave has a Load, Control requirement, Flow requirements, range, duration and effect.

1. Confirm the caster has embraced the Source, knows the weave, and meets its prerequisites.
2. Add its Load to existing committed Load. This total must fit Current Capacity, even for an instant cast.
3. Check that Strain plus Load fits **twice Current Capacity**. If it does not, reject the cast without spending anything.
4. Spend one action and add Load to **Strain**, even if the cast fails. Strain starts at 0.
5. If the resulting Strain exceeds Current Capacity, add **2** to the difficulty for that cast.
6. Roll only when uncertainty has a meaningful consequence. Safe practice spends time and Strain, but needs no roll.

A sustained weave commits its Load until released. The total committed Load cannot exceed Current Capacity. Sustain at most **Control** weaves at once. Release a weave freely. Each new cast spends Strain once; sustaining does not spend Strain each exchange. The same weave cannot stack its effect on the same target.

A ten-minute recovery break removes **2 Strain**. Its end must be at least 60 minutes after the previous restorative break ended. The first break has no cooldown. The same break can restore Vitality. Eight hours of adequate rest clears Strain. Summarise safe practice in time blocks; do not require repeated recovery messages.

A failed cast can fizzle, expose the caster, cost time or create a stated local hazard. It never secretly reduces Potential, removes channeling, or kills the character. Permanent channeling risk requires a separate, informed choice before the dangerous attempt, with the exact risk stated. Normal play never requires that choice.

### Basic channeling operations

The following procedures are house rules. Their setting descriptions need lore review before release.

- **Embrace:** one action in conflict, without a roll for a trained character. Outside conflict, allow a brief moment. Embracing alone adds no Strain.
- **Release:** free on the character's turn. It ends all held weaves. It cannot retrospectively cancel a consequence already resolved.
- **Concentration:** after taking harm, drop one held weave of the player's choice. The player can instead spend 1 Strain, within its limit. Apply this once per exchange, without another roll.
- **Incapacitation:** end all held weaves and release the Source immediately. Already paid Strain remains spent. Tied weaves follow their declared duration.
- **Reinforce a held weave:** use one action and pay its Load again in Strain. Reuse its existing committed Load and slot. Do not add another copy. Air Binding uses this procedure for later conflict progress.
- **Tie off:** requires Control 3 and a weave marked as eligible. After casting, spend another action and 1 Strain. The weave then lasts one hour without concentration. It still commits its Load, but occupies no held-weave slot. Maintain at most Control tied weaves. Their effects do not stack. Untying requires one action within the weave's original range.

Tying eligibility comes from each [catalogue entry](weave-catalogue.md). The six core entries permit Warning Ward and Elemental Guard. The expanded catalogue also permits Lamp. Tying still requires Control 3 and the stated extra action and Strain.

### Casts longer than one action

A long cast requires more than the ordinary ten-second action. Its stated duration includes the opening action. Do not add another action's time at completion.

Long casts cannot start during active conflict. First establish a safe interval without an immediate opposing threat. If active conflict begins, interrupt the cast before resolving its first exchange. A long cast never suppresses opposing exchanges or creates extra enemy turns.

Before starting, validate the learned form, target, required contact, range, Load, held slots and every possible resource cost. Pay the full casting Strain once. Reserve its Load and one held slot until completion, even if its eventual effect is instantaneous.

Record the form, target, start second, required casting seconds and elapsed casting seconds. Only one long cast can be in progress per caster. It counts alongside other held and tied Load. Its reserved slot counts alongside held slots.

The caster cannot start another cast, move, attack, defend or sleep while continuing the cast. Those actions interrupt it. Brief speech and releasing another held weave are allowed. Rules questions use no fictional time.

Required contact and range must remain valid throughout. Losing either interrupts the cast. Incapacitation or releasing the Source also interrupts it. When harm causes a concentration check, treat the pending cast as one held effect. Apply the ordinary choice to drop an effect or pay 1 Strain. No additional die roll is required.

An interruption ends the pending cast and frees its reserved Load and slot. Already spent Strain and elapsed time remain spent. There is no partial magical effect or automatic refund. A new attempt starts from zero with a new cost. Training-day credit and later recovery periods follow their own retention rules.

Advance time only to the next relevant world event, resource boundary or required player decision. Resolve an interruption before advancing further. A cast completes only after all its required seconds pass without interruption. Resolve its frozen outcome then.

At completion, apply the effect and release the reservation for an instantaneous weave. For a sustained effect, convert the reservation into its held Load and slot. Do not charge Strain or a second slot again.

Additional outcome time is follow-up work after resolution. Record its remaining seconds if interrupted. It is not rest, study or paid work. Finish that obligation before starting another weave. A continuing emergency can interrupt the work, but cannot erase its remaining time.

For example, Wound Treatment requires 3,600 casting seconds. Interruption after 1,200 seconds retains that elapsed time and the spent 4 Strain. It produces no treatment. Completion instead starts its separate patient-recovery period. An interrupted recovery period retains credit under that entry; it does not repeat the successful cast.

### Advanced operations and their limits

These conservative numbers define optional modules. They need a worked example and lore review before entering play. The required rank route does not depend on them.

**Shielding:** requires a learned Shield form, Control 3, Spirit 2 and Load 3. Range is 10 paces; duration is sustained. Against an unwilling channeler, use a four-point progress track. Difficulty is 14 against Control 1–2, 16 against Control 3, or 18 against Control 4. Each action pays Load again, using the existing shield slot. Clean adds 2 progress; costly adds 1 and the declared counter-cost. A setback adds none and applies the counter-cost. At four progress, the target cannot embrace or cast, and its held weaves end. Ending the shield restores access. It does not cause severing or reduce Potential.

Add 2 difficulty if the target has greater Current Capacity or already holds the Source. These two conditions do not stack. This adjustment replaces the ordinary obstacle penalty for that check. When struggling free, add 2 difficulty if the shield caster has greater Current Capacity. Declare all applicable conditions before rolling. These adjustments are house rules that require lore review.

A shielded target can struggle using Will + Control against the shield caster's same difficulty formula. Use a separate four-point track. A clean result adds 2; a costly result adds 1 with a declared cost. Completion ends the shield. Struggling is not a cast and costs no Strain. Both parties retain physical actions and escape choices. Apply the six-exchange limit to the whole conflict, not separately to each track.

**Small circle:** the initial supported limit is three willing participants. This is a content limit, not a canonical maximum. Choose one leader; all members must be trained and embrace. Formation takes one action per member. The leader must know each cast form. Use the leader's Will and Control. For each Flow prerequisite, use the highest participating Flow rating.

Before joining, release all individual held weaves. A participant must have no committed tied Load. Individual casting remains unavailable until the circle ends. This prevents counting one person's capacity twice.

Circle Capacity equals the largest member's Current Capacity, plus 2 per additional member, to a maximum of 12. Declare the Load allocation before a cast. Divide its Strain cost into whole points among members; each must remain within their own Strain limit. A contributing member above their own fatigue threshold adds 2 difficulty, once total. Only the leader acts with the circle each exchange. Other members cannot also cast or grant support during that exchange.

Committed circle Load must fit Circle Capacity. Use the leader's Control for held-weave slots. When a member takes harm, apply concentration to one circle weave, or that member pays 1 Strain. A member's incapacitation ends the circle and its held weaves. The leader ends the link if any member requests it under the default campaign agreement. There is no involuntary linking. Tying circle weaves and larger circles are outside the initial module.

**Angreal:** the initial supported item adds 2 to the holder's Load limit and committed-Load limit. It does not raise Control, Flow ratings or Potential. Strain limits and fatigue thresholds still use natural Current Capacity. Use at most one such item. Attunement takes one safe hour. If removing it would leave excess held Load, choose which held weaves end before removal. Tied Load cannot depend on the item. Circle and angreal combinations need their own reviewed module. Qualification uses natural Current Capacity; no item is required.

**Talents and dangerous exceptions:** no random Talent is required for promotion. Special Talents, sa'angreal, severing and overchanneling are unavailable until a specific module is defined and reviewed. It must give prerequisites, exact costs, outcomes and any irreversible risk. The GM cannot improvise these rules during an action. Advanced Healing needs a reviewed variant before it can remove Wounds or exceed the initial recovery limits.

## 5 Six initial weave entries

These are the initial mechanically complete examples. They are not a claim that the full 40-weave adventure catalogue is written. Their names and numerical effects are campaign game terms. The lore review must confirm how each effect is described in the setting.

Use the same core test for all six. Against an opponent, use that opponent's Guard as the difficulty. For other uncertain uses, use 14, 18 or 22 based on the actual conditions. A safe routine use needs no roll.

| Weave | Requirements | Load; duration; range | Defined effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spark | Control 1; Fire 1 | 1; instant; 10 paces | Ignite a small exposed dry fuel source. It does not ignite a person or wet material automatically. An attack removes 1 Resistance. |
| Air Hand | Control 1; Air 1 | 1; sustained; 10 paces | Move up to 5 kg slowly. It can retrieve a visible object or manipulate an accessible simple latch. It cannot open an unknown lock or restrain a resisting person. An improvised attack removes 1 Resistance. |
| Air Binding | Control 2; Air 2, Spirit 1 | 2; sustained; 10 paces | Restrain one ordinary target. During conflict, each successful application removes 2 Resistance. At 0 Resistance the target is restrained while the weave remains sustained. Before that point the target can still oppose the caster. An unusually large or powerful target can require a different method, declared before casting. |
| Warning Ward | Control 2; Spirit 2, Air 1 | 2; sustained; area up to 10 paces across, placed within 10 paces | Warn the caster when a physical creature crosses the boundary. It gives no identity or thoughts. If the warning allows a response, give +2 to one relevant surprise or escape test. It does not cancel an attack automatically. |
| Elemental Guard | Control 2; Air 2, Earth 1, Fire 1, Water 1 | 2; sustained; self or one person within 5 paces | Reduce harm from projectiles, falling debris or ordinary flame by 1 per exchange. It does not reduce melee harm or every form of channeling. It can reduce harm to 0. |
| Healing | Control 3; Water 2, Spirit 2, Air 1 | 3; instant; touch | Restore 4 Vitality to another person, once per patient between full rests. It cannot heal the caster, restore the dead or remove Wounds in this ruleset. First aid and ordinary rest remain useful. |

For later entries, use this fixed schema. Include **name, description, source, Control, required Flows, Load, range, duration, targets, exact effect, difficulty, costly result, setback, prohibited uses and learning conditions**. Group the 40-entry catalogue into foundation, field, defence, conflict and advanced options. Declare any exceptional subsystem before its first use.

The formal training curriculum is a separate collection of assessed forms. Do not ask the player to perform or record a hundred individual rolls. The adventure catalogue is not a hidden requirement to know every formal test form.

Track Resistance removed by Air Binding separately from other reductions. Reinforcement adds to that same contribution. If the Binding ends, restore only its contribution, up to the foe's original Resistance. Apply concentration after simultaneous harm, then check whether the target remains restrained. A Binding that ends on the decisive exchange cannot preserve the restraint.

A Binding contribution can establish restraint only. Restoration cannot reverse independently established surrender, incapacitation, or death. Combined physical and Binding progress cannot turn restraint into a lethal effect.

## 6 Conflict harm and recovery

Keep conflict small. An ordinary lone foe has **Guard 14, Resistance 6 and Harm 2**. A dangerous foe has **Guard 18, Resistance 8 and Harm 3**. A weak obstacle can use Resistance 4 and Harm 1. Describe other foes with these three visible numbers before a direct confrontation. A group can share one Resistance track; a group does not automatically get one attack per member.

One exchange is about ten seconds. The player states one action. Use one roll to resolve the exchange; do not roll a second attack for the foe.

Use this order for every exchange:

1. Declare intent, time, difficulty, costs, and all outcome bands.
2. Validate prerequisites, range, Load, and projected resource costs.
3. Let the player commit. Spend the declared action and costs.
4. Resolve the roll and any permitted ally support.
5. Apply the selected band's harm and effect simultaneously.
6. Resolve incapacitation, concentration, and effects that end.
7. Check the resulting state and advance fictional time.
8. Save the event before delivering its narration.

There are no additional reaction turns. Foe attacks are included in the exchange's declared consequences. For a pure defence, use Grace plus Athletics against Guard. A clean result avoids harm; a costly result halves harm, rounded down. A setback applies full harm. This defence rule also covers an embrace action under attack.

For range calculations, one pace equals one metre as a game convention. An action can include up to five paces of unopposed movement. Crossing a threatened area uses the exchange's action and declared outcome bands. Leaving the conflict uses the escape procedure below.

| Exchange result | Effect for a direct attack |
|---|---|
| Clean | Remove the action's damage from the foe's Resistance. Take no counter-harm. |
| Costly | Remove that damage and take the foe's Harm. |
| Setback | Take the foe's Harm; remove no Resistance. |

An ordinary weapon removes **2 Resistance**. An improvised weapon removes **1**. A heavy weapon removes **3**, needs Body 2, and uses both hands. Resistance measures the ability to keep fighting; reaching 0 can mean surrender, disarmament, restraint or incapacitation. Killing is not the automatic result. The player must state a lethal intention before the exchange.

Other actions use the same outcome bands. A clean escape reaches safety. A costly escape reaches safety with the stated harm or loss. A failed escape changes the situation and offers a new choice. A guard action uses the pure defence rule. Guarding alone does not reduce Resistance or stop an external clock.

More complex goals use a public **4-point progress track**. A clean action adds 2 progress. A costly action adds 1 and applies the stated cost. A setback applies its cost. The opponent can be an obstacle instead of a person. State what completing the track achieves before the first roll. Do not add a second track merely to make an already won objective take longer.

Subtract harm from Vitality. At 0, the character falls and gains **one Wound**, to a maximum of three. Stop the fight for that character. In the default campaign, defeat produces a stated consequence such as capture, rescue debt, delay or a lost objective. It does not cause surprise death or permanent loss of the rank route. Three Wounds require rest and care before another field operation.

A ten-minute break restores **2 Vitality**, at most once per hour. A full eight-hour rest restores all Vitality. First aid takes ten minutes and restores **2 Vitality once per person per full rest**, with a Medicine test only if conditions make treatment uncertain. A Wound heals after **three days with care**, or **seven days without care**. Heal Wounds one at a time. Healing magic in the initial catalogue does not skip this injury time. Wounds do not also add a hidden combat penalty.

Armour is optional equipment, not a starting requirement. A suitable protective kit reduces one declared source of harm by 1, at most once per exchange. It does not stack with Elemental Guard against the same harm. Neither defence can increase damage or create extra attacks. The GM states mobility or social limits before the kit is chosen.

## 7 Allies and resources

Each established ally has a name, motive, two areas of competence, a boundary and a Bond rating from **0 to 3**. Bond records the ally's trust in the character. It does not assign the player's feelings or grant a universal roll bonus. A distinct consequential kept or broken commitment can change Bond by at most one. Record its evidence. Gifts and repeated chat cannot farm Bond. Bond never gates promotion.

A willing ally has **two support uses**. Reset the remaining uses to two after a full eight-hour rest in safety. Never add uses above two. A support use requires a credible action in an area of competence. Choose one:

- Give +2 before one roll.
- Reduce one instance of harm by 2, after the result is known.
- Complete one routine parallel task that frees the player to do something else.

At most one ally support use can affect an exchange. Several allies do not form a compulsory bonus stack. Each ally can also provide information, access or personal choices through the fiction. Those contributions follow their knowledge and motives, not an unlimited support charge.

Allies can disagree or leave. A unique ally never owns the only route to a required lesson or rank proof. Solo preparation can supply the same +2 on a suitable test, but it costs a scene, time or a resource. A prepared solo character still faces decisions that an ally could have made easier.

Start with Supply 4; the maximum is 6. A Supply unit supports a group of up to four for one full day away from normal provision. Spend it once per exposed calendar day, including camping, study, and rest. Two Supply cost 1 silver coin. Basic institutional food, housing and required training are provided while the character remains enrolled. Ordinary paid work earns 1 silver coin per full day when such work is available. Safe work within the character's competence needs no roll. Money cannot buy advancement points.

At Supply 0, mark the shortage and use the next travel scene to obtain provision or return to safety. A full rest without food and water restores only half maximum Vitality and removes 2 Strain. A shortage does not silently cause permanent damage. Longer deprivation needs a clearly stated new situation.

Use a visible calendar. Record travel days, training weeks and recovery days. Threat clocks have four or six segments and a published trigger. Time does not pass just because the player asks a rules question or sends another message. Rest can advance a clock only when that clock's trigger includes elapsed time.

### Travel and daily activity

A day permits one main activity of up to eight hours: travel, paid work, study or wound recovery. Full rest needs eight other hours. The remaining hours cover meals, chores and minor actions. A full training day cannot also count as a travel, work or wound-recovery day. A training week means five full study days within seven calendar days. Interruptions retain completed study days.

Full rest requires release of all held weaves. Tied weaves expire at their recorded time. Sleeping does not preserve a held weave.

Wound recovery days count only when recovery is the main activity. With care, remove one Wound after three such days; otherwise, use seven. Ordinary full rest still restores Vitality and Strain on other days. First aid and ten-minute breaks consume their actual time.

On a suitable route, walking covers 24 km per travel day. Riding a supplied, fit mount covers 40 km. Rough terrain halves those rates. Severe weather halves them again. The route record must state these conditions before travel. Distance is recorded in whole kilometres. A partial trip takes `ceil(480 × distance / daily rate)` minutes, up to eight hours per day. Unused time does not create a second full training day.

An exposed calendar day costs one Supply per group of up to four people. Larger groups spend `ceil(people / 4)` Supply per day. Do not charge twice for two travel segments on the same day. A supplied stop can replace that day's consumption only if it provides the whole day's needs for the group.

## 8 Rank is earned separately

General Level is not rank. Current Capacity is not rank. Knowing a rare weave is not rank. Each rank needs published technical, judgement and service proofs. The exact scenes belong in the chapter plan.

**Proposed Accepted readiness, after chapter 4:** Control 2, at least three Flows at 2, Current Capacity 5, and the published novice curriculum and judgement proofs. These channel ratings cost 6 points, against 8 earned by chapter 4.

**Proposed Aes Sedai technical readiness, after chapter 10:** Control 4, every Flow at least 2, Current Capacity 8, and the published technical curriculum. The channel rating cost is **17 points**, against **20 earned by chapter 10**. Chapter 11 completes the published final service and judgement proofs. No ordinary Attribute minimum, ally, rare talent or Potential above 8 is required.

Before either readiness review, the player may freely reassign Channel Points after one week of bridging instruction. Keep learned weave knowledge, but obey its current prerequisites. This protects the route if the player chose attractive optional training. The GM must disclose that choice before the review; do not create an extra grind or a fee.

Rehearsals and readiness reviews can be repeated. They give specific feedback and retain completed proofs. If the character is not ready, provide a bounded bridge scene and another review opportunity. A new unrelated subplot is not a mandatory bridge.

**Open campaign house rule:** once the stated readiness proofs are complete, technical completion of the formal rank test is assured. Play the formal test in three to five substantial scenes. Tests within those scenes determine costs, relationships and the character's decisions; they do not randomly revoke the earned qualification. This is a deliberate protective adaptation, not a claim about the novels' institutional rules. Do not pretend that repeated failed formal tests are routine canon.

Begin the formal test rested, at full Vitality, with no Wounds. Rehearsal confirms that the technical workload fits the candidate's resources. Use assessment mode during the formal test. Do not charge expedition Strain for each curriculum form. Declare each scene's permitted cost before resolving it. No cost can stop completion or remove the earned qualification. Apply physical injury and exhaustion after completion, with at most one Wound from the whole formal test. Relationship changes, obligations and recovery time can still matter. Do not use these costs as a new promotion gate.

The player can knowingly refuse a rank or abandon the route. Permanent-risk mode is separate and requires an explicit choice after the exact consequences are explained. An ambiguous roleplay statement, one failed die roll, an NPC's dislike or a missed optional episode does not count as that choice.

Under this rule, a ready character's risk of **unchosen permanent route loss during the formal test is exactly 0%**. This follows from the rule excluding that outcome. It is not an empirical success estimate for the AI, implementation or whole campaign. A rule violation remains an implementation failure that needs correction.

## 9 Pacing and numerical checks

Use four substantial sessions per chapter as the planning baseline: **48 sessions** across 12 chapters. The target range of 40–60 needs an average of 3⅓–5 sessions per chapter. It is a planning target, not a statistical promise.

The chapter names below describe functions, not a fixed plot. Agree the specific proofs at each chapter's start. Use several methods for each proof. The baseline column gives cumulative sessions; it never triggers an award.

| Chapter | Function and required result | Capacity after proof | Baseline sessions |
|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | Foundations: safe practice, a recorded learning result and a small service task. | 3 | 4 |
| 2 | Shared work: a first capacity proof and a task with competing responsibilities. | 4 | 8 |
| 3 | First field task: prepare, choose a method and return with a verified result. | 4 | 12 |
| 4 | Novice review: second capacity proof, novice curriculum and judgement review. | 5 | 16 |
| 5 | Accepted test: three to five substantial scenes; choose the next study direction. | 5 | 20 |
| 6 | Accepted fieldwork: third capacity proof and a useful service result. | 6 | 24 |
| 7 | Independent study: complete an investigation and explain its evidence. | 6 | 28 |
| 8 | Responsibility: fourth capacity proof and a decision affecting other people. | 7 | 32 |
| 9 | Advanced practice: demonstrate broad Flow control and resolve a chosen project. | 7 | 36 |
| 10 | Technical review: fifth capacity proof and the full technical curriculum. | 8 | 40 |
| 11 | Final service: complete the published independent service and judgement proofs. | 8 | 44 |
| 12 | Formal test and shawl: three to five substantial scenes, rank choices and resolution. | 8 | 48 |
